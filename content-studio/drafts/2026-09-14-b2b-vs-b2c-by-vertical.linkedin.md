## Post

In education, winning a B2B customer costs 7.3x more to acquire than winning a B2C one — $1,143 vs $156, First Page Sage's 2026 B2B and B2C acquisition-cost reports. That's the widest gap this dataset supports inside one vertical, not across two different ones.

Every vertical has its own number: ecommerce is only 1.3x ($86 vs $66). Real estate is 4.8x. A blended "B2B costs more" average erases the range that matters if you're budgeting for one specific market. (There's a flashier 13x in the same data — but that one's B2B education against B2B wholesale/ecommerce, same buyer type, different vertical, not a B2B-vs-B2C comparison at all. Worth knowing which one you're citing.)

Second trap in the same data: "conversion rate" isn't one metric. The SaaS/software category converts at 3.8% on a landing page (Unbounce) and 7.6% site-wide (Ruler Analytics) — both true, neither split by B2B vs. B2C, measuring two different funnel stages that a lot of benchmark decks quietly average together.

I built this as a living artifact instead of a static chart: pick one of 10 industries, pick a metric — acquisition cost, conversion, paid search, sales-cycle length, email, review reliance — and B2B/B2C replay side by side, sourced, with real gaps shown as "no source," never guessed.

The image attached here is the rudimentary version — one frozen comparison. The interactive one lives on the site: pick your own vertical, rank all 10 by whatever metric you care about, and check the source on every number.

https://mitchjmiller.com/blog/b2b-vs-b2c-by-vertical/

Share this with someone about to build a "B2B vs B2C" slide from one benchmark study that doesn't say which funnel stage it measured.
