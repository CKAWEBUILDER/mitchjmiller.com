# Growth Engineering, Without the Job Title

*By Mitchell Miller · ~5 min read · growth systems / applied data / agent operations*

## The title

Recruiters search by title. So I went looking for mine.

"Growth Operations Engineer" is what the work sounds like. It is also three departments stapled into one phrase, and almost no recruiter types it into a search box.

Growth engineer is closer. In [The Pragmatic Engineer's definition](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/what-is-growth-engineering), it means writing code to help a company make more money, mostly inside the product: onboarding, referral loops, experiments. GTM engineer is closer still: the revenue plumbing, the CRM, the enrichment, the routing. Data engineer, growth: the pipelines underneath all of it.

Each of those is a room. The work is the hallway.

## The work

Here is the plain version.

Research the market, the competitors and the technology. Measure what is happening with statistics that survive a second look. Turn that into a data-driven growth strategy across search, answer engine optimization, conversion, paid and social. Turn the strategy into architecture: content, interface, engineering. Build the front end, and enough of the back end to make it real. Report it to executives, managers and clients in language they can act on. Then revise, because the target is immediate impact balanced against growth that lasts.

Big data in. Simple, statistically sound decisions out. Everything else is delivery.

## Prove it, then predict it

A statistician audits the past: design the sample, control the bias, prove why something happened, attach the confidence interval. A data scientist builds the machine that guesses what happens next, at a scale no proof can reach.

Most companies keep the statistician and the data scientist in separate departments. Search taught me to run them as one motion. Prove what moved the number. Predict what moves it next. Build the thing. Measure whether the prediction held.

The [SFC Surf School](/case-studies/sfc-surf-school/) case is the small, complete version. 973 pages crawled across ten competing businesses before a word was rewritten. Search clicks from 5 to 54 and impressions from 470 to 1,652 in the comparison window. And a ten-break interactive surf explorer that launched after that window, which the case study says out loud rather than quietly taking the credit.

That last sentence is the whole discipline.

## Scale

The same loop, larger. At [Dignity Health and CommonSpirit Health](/case-studies/commonspirit-network-consolidation/) it ran as enterprise SEO through a merger: sixty-three-plus sites consolidated, twenty-plus enterprise migrations, organic sessions from 2.65M to 4.9M+, and a [thousand-location entity system](/case-studies/commonspirit-locations-conversion-engine/) that later reporting tied to $15.21M in FY22 revenue. At [Apple](/case-studies/apple-seasonal-search/) it ran across the Americas region's seasonal commerce. At [Stanford Health Care](/case-studies/stanford-myhealth-seo/) it ran inside regulated healthcare, paid and organic together.

Different rooms. Same hallway.

## One person, squared

Now the part no title can hold.

I no longer do this alone, and I do not do it with a payroll. The team is multi-agent: agents and subagents, dozens on an ordinary night, around a hundred on a heavy one. Each has a persona, a role and a set of skills. A researcher that returns sourced findings and never prose. A designer that builds the visual from the researcher's data. A critic that blocks a piece of work from finishing until it clears a checklist. A reviewer whose only job is to break whatever the builders call done. A release owner, exactly one per project. A coordinator that checks in every ninety minutes and stops opening new work once usage crosses a line I set.

The harnesses vary: Claude Code, Codex, a self-hosted router, open-weight models next in line for the small, private tasks. The records do not. Every project keeps one plain-text source of truth in Git that any agent reads before touching anything and updates before it stops. And the playbooks rewrite themselves. At the end of a session the agents record what worked, what failed and which rule changed, so the next session starts further along than the last one did. The technique behind that loop is mine and stays mine. Its effect is visible in the work.

One night this September: two coordinators, eight lanes, six projects. By morning, a fifty-seven-page site rebuilt as complete HTML and live, a free data tool shipped, and three real defects found by the reviewer and fixed with regression tests before I woke up. Nothing sent, booked or bought without me.

That is what squared means.

## FAQ

**Growth engineer vs GTM engineer: what is the difference?**
A growth engineer writes product code that acquires and retains users: onboarding, experiments, referral loops. A GTM engineer builds the revenue systems around the product: CRM, enrichment, routing, automation. This work spans both and adds the research and measurement that decide what either one should build.

**Statistician vs data scientist: which does growth work need?**
Both, in sequence. Statistical discipline proves what moved a number and with how much confidence. Data science predicts what moves it next, at scale. The step most teams skip is the third one: build the thing and measure whether the prediction held.

**Are the agent teams people?**
No. They are AI agents and subagents with defined roles, personas and skills, run under written rules, with one human principal accountable on every engagement. Specialist people are brought in per engagement rather than kept on a roster.
