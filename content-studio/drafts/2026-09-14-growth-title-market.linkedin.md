**Hook**

One job board says "Growth Operations Engineer" has zero openings in the United States. Another says 10,000+.

Same three words. Same day.

---

**Body**

I counted ten growth, SEO and AI-search job titles on 14 September 2026, twice each: once as an exact phrase, once as a loose keyword search. Then I pulled every published salary figure for each one and refused to average them.

The takeaway I did not expect:

**"Director of SEO" returns 19 exact-phrase postings in the entire United States, and zero on Built In — against LinkedIn's 3,000+ for the same words. A factor of 158.**
(SimplyHired exact-phrase US search and LinkedIn public guest search, both retrieved 14 September 2026.)

Neither number is a lie. They answer different questions, and nothing on either page tells you which one you are reading. Every "X thousand jobs available" figure in a careers article inherits that problem.

The thing worth acting on sits one layer down: published pay disagrees most on the titles with the fewest real postings. Two sources put "Growth Engineer" at $101,752 and $359,824 — 3.5x apart, and the higher one rests on 38 self-reported salaries. The rarer the title, the less its advertised pay means.

Meanwhile, in a study of 328,650 marketing postings, the 720 that named AI search, GEO or AEO in the title were 28% leadership roles against 15% for standard SEO titles — and carried a 20.5% median pay premium.

The vocabulary has not settled yet. That is the whole opportunity.
