# Search Results by Intent: Five Different Pages, 8 of 25 Measurements Missing

*By Mitchell Miller · ~4 min read · AI search / measurement*

## TL;DR — three numbers

- **68.01%** of US Google searches ended with no click at all in January–April 2026, up from 60.45% two years earlier. *(SparkToro, Similarweb clickstream panel, 9 June 2026.)*
- When an AI Overview appears, **position-one organic CTR falls 58%** — nearly double the 34.5% the same team measured eight months before. *(Ahrefs, 300,000 keywords via Search Console, 4 February 2026.)*
- Informational queries trigger an AI Overview **36%** of the time. Transactional queries: **5%**. *(Seer Interactive, 5.47M queries, 24 April 2026.)*

Those three numbers are usually quoted as one story about "search". They are three different pages.

## "Informational" is not one number

Three credible panels measured AI Overview presence on informational queries and got **36%**, **69.8%** and **92%**. Same label, 56 points apart, because one panel is 5.47M mixed queries, one is 10,000 desktop keywords from December 2024, and one is 540 hand-collected queries inside local-service verticals.

That spread is not noise to be averaged away — it is the finding. A trigger rate is a property of a keyword set, not of the internet. The artifact prints the disagreement next to the number instead of picking a winner, because a number you can defend in a meeting needs its panel attached.

## The click you lose by not being cited

Seer measured the same keyword in three states. No AI Overview on the page: **3.35%** organic CTR. AI Overview present and you are cited in it: **2.07%**. AI Overview present and you are not: **0.94%** — a 3.6× drop from the plain page.

Citation is worth roughly 120% more organic clicks per impression than presence without it. That is the whole practical case for answer-engine work, stated as one measurement rather than a philosophy.

## Where the answer box still is not

Local intent is the outlier. Whitespark hand-collected 540 queries across Houston, Phoenix and Denver: **15%** carried an AI Overview, **93%** carried a local pack. Informational queries in the same study: 92% AI Overview, 6% local pack.

Transactional is the other quiet corner — **5%** in Seer's panel, and the only intent measured going *backwards*: AI Overview presence on transactional pages fell **5%** between November 2025 and April 2026, while commercial-investigation pages rose **71%**. Google is chasing the shortlist, not the checkout.

## The direction of travel is the shortlist

Narrow commercial investigation to its most common format and the rate inverts completely: **95.4%** of "X vs Y" comparison queries trigger an AI Overview — the highest rate in this dataset. Meanwhile paid took the clicks that organic lost: in the US headphones category, paid click share went 16% → 36% in a year while organic went 73% → 50%.

Comparison content is simultaneously the most answered and the most contested surface in search. Both things are measured; neither is optional to know.

## The payoff: the model is older than the problem

The intent split the industry still quotes — 80% informational, 10% navigational, 10% transactional — was measured in **2008**, on 1.5M queries, at 74% classification accuracy, before mobile. No full-sample replacement exists. Commercial investigation and local intent have no modern share-of-searches figure at all.

It gets thinner. People Also Ask, featured snippets and ad presence have no by-intent measurement in any study I could find — only all-query averages, and the most recent primary People Also Ask figure is from 2020. Of the 25 intent-by-measure cells in the artifact, **17 carry a source and eight are empty**, and the empty ones are drawn as gaps rather than filled with a plausible guess.

We segment strategy by intent every day. The published measurement covers two thirds of the grid, and the gaps are rarely labelled as gaps.

## How to read these numbers

- **Scopes differ.** Seer is 5.47M queries over Jan 2025–Feb 2026. Ahrefs is 300,000 keywords, Dec 2025 against a Dec 2023 baseline. Whitespark is 540 queries collected by hand in May 2025. They are not interchangeable, and the artifact dates every one.
- **One widely-repeated stat is excluded.** A claimed Semrush study of "18.4 billion categorized queries across 142 countries" finding 57.3% informational intent appears word-for-word across several AI-generated statistics roundups, with no primary article, author or methodology behind it. It is not used here, and it should not be used anywhere.
- **One disagreement is deliberately not in the headline.** First Page Sage reports that AI Overviews have minimal impact on organic CTR, which directly contradicts Ahrefs and Seer. Given the sample sizes and methodology disclosure, the weight sits with Ahrefs and Seer — but the contradiction is real and worth naming rather than hiding.
- **A widely-quoted number did not survive the check.** "People Also Ask appears on 64.9% of searches" is repeated across statistics roundups attributed to "Semrush", but no Semrush page states it: the trail ends at a roundup citing another roundup. Semrush's own People Also Ask study — 1,000,000 US desktop keywords — measured **49.37% on desktop and 52.27% on mobile**, and it was published in **September 2020**. The artifact prints the 2020 figure with its date on the bar, and the 64.9% is gone.
- **One figure is still a secondary citation** (the featured-snippet decline). It is labelled as such inside the artifact.

## FAQ

**How often does an AI Overview appear, by search intent?**
It depends on the panel. Seer Interactive's 5.47M-query study (24 April 2026) measured 36% for informational, 8% for commercial investigation and 5% for transactional. Whitespark measured 15% for local intent and 92% for informational inside local-service verticals (12 May 2025). Semrush put navigational at 10.33% by October 2025, up from 0.84% in January. Quote the number with its study, not on its own.

**Do AI Overviews reduce organic clicks?**
The two largest disclosed samples say yes, clearly. Ahrefs measured a 58% drop in position-one CTR on AI-Overview keywords (300,000 keywords, Search Console, February 2026). Seer measured 0.94% organic CTR when an AI Overview is present and the brand is not cited, against 3.35% with no AI Overview. First Page Sage disagrees, but does not disclose a sample size.

**What share of searches is informational in 2026?**
No modern full-sample answer has been published. The 80/10/10 split still in wide circulation comes from a 2008 study of 1.5M queries. A 2025 clickstream report put navigational nearer 30%, but only through a secondary citation. Treat any confident 2026 intent split as an estimate until someone publishes the panel.
