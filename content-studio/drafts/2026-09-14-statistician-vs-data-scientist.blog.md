# Statistician vs Data Scientist — and Two Jobs With No BLS Code

*By Mitchell Miller · ~4 min read · labor data / applied statistics*

## TL;DR

- Data scientist employment is projected to grow **35%** from 2025–2035 — more than 3x statisticians' **11%**. ([BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/data-scientists.htm), 2025 edition)
- Data scientists out-earn statisticians by **$14,600** a year at the national median ([$120,230](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/data-scientists.htm) vs [$105,650](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/mathematicians-and-statisticians.htm)) — but that flips inside computer systems design: statisticians **$167,180**, data scientists **$132,380** (same two BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook pages, industry pay tables).
- **Growth engineer and AI engineer have no BLS occupation code at all** — no SOC classification, no wage survey, no growth projection — even though AI engineer out-posts every other title on every job board checked. ([BLS SOC lookup guidance](https://www.bls.gov/soc/notices/2022/finding_soc_code.htm))

## The gap in the data

Statistician and data scientist each carry a BLS SOC code, an annual OEWS wage table and an Occupational Outlook Handbook page. Growth engineer and AI engineer carry neither — no code, no wage survey, no official growth projection exists for either title, confirmed against BLS's own SOC-lookup guidance. That's not a gap in this research; it's a gap in the federal government's occupational taxonomy. The artifact above flags it live: pick either of those two roles and every BLS-sourced row on the page renders "No data" instead of a guess.

## What each role pays

Data scientists earn [**$120,230**](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/data-scientists.htm) a year at the national median against statisticians' [**$105,650**](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/mathematicians-and-statisticians.htm) (BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, 2025). But "the" data-scientist salary depends on which 2025 survey you read: Stack Overflow's global sample says **$82,910**; a US-only re-cut of that dataset says **$160,000**; BLS lands between. ([2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/work)) None of those is wrong — different scopes, not different facts, and averaging them would hide that. Growth engineer has no compensation survey tracking the title directly at all.

## Who's growing faster

Data scientist employment is projected to grow **35%** from 2025 to 2035; statistician, **11%**. ([BLS Employment Projections](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/mathematicians-and-statisticians.htm)) Growth engineer and AI engineer have no projection to cite — the closest BLS-tracked cousin to "AI engineer," Computer and Information Research Scientists, is projected at 22%, and it is explicitly a proxy, not a measurement of AI engineer itself.

## The education gate

**62%** of statisticians hold a master's degree as their highest credential; data scientists split **48%** bachelor's and **44%** master's — a materially lower graduate bar. ([O*NET OnLine](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-2051.00)) Neither growth engineer nor AI engineer has an O*NET occupational profile to pull an education distribution from.

## Prove why, or predict what's next

Underneath the pay and growth numbers is a practice difference that explains most of the rest. A statistician's work is inference: design the study, quantify the uncertainty, defend the result under scrutiny — usually against data someone else already curated. A data scientist's work is prediction: build and ship a model optimized for forecast accuracy, usually after cleaning the messy data themselves. ([Yardstick](https://yardstick.team/compare-roles/what-is-the-difference-between-data-scientist-and-statistician)) One is hypothesis-driven; the other, exploratory. ([Indeed Career Advice](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/finding-a-job/data-scientist-vs-statistician)) Prove why the number moved, or predict what moves it next — that split, more than the job titles, is what the pay and growth gaps above are actually pricing.

## How to read these numbers

Two BLS products disagree on purpose, not by error. Occupational Outlook Handbook employment counts run 5–8% higher than the raw OEWS survey estimate for the same occupation-year — OOH's Employment Projections program adjusts the OEWS number rather than repeating it, so both are legitimate. Survey pay figures carry the same caveat at a larger scale: a self-reported developer survey, a US-only cut of it, and a government wage census measure overlapping but different populations — why data-scientist pay above spans **$82,910** to **$160,000** before BLS's figure even enters the range. The artifact's sources drawer labels every number with its specific product and date rather than blending them into one line.

## FAQ

**Is a data scientist the same as a statistician?** No. Statisticians work hypothesis-driven inference on curated data; data scientists work exploratory prediction on data they usually clean themselves, per BLS occupational data and Indeed/Yardstick's role comparisons.

**Which pays more, statistician or data scientist?** Data scientist, at the national median ([$120,230](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/data-scientists.htm) vs [$105,650](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/mathematicians-and-statisticians.htm)) — but statistician pays more specifically inside computer systems design ($167,180 vs $132,380, same two BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook pages' industry tables).

**Do growth engineer and AI engineer have official job classifications?** No. Neither has a BLS SOC occupation code, wage table or growth projection; job-board volume and practitioner surveys are the only available signal for either title.
