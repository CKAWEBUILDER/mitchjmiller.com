# Notes — gaps, blocked pages, disagreements
Research window: 2026-09-14, ~90-minute time-box. Read against content-studio/CLAUDE.md house rules (no fabricated stats, cite everything) — anything below a reasonable confidence bar is flagged here rather than smoothed over in data.json.

## Structural gap: half the requested roles have no BLS data at all
The assignment asks for BLS OOH/OES stats on all four roles. **Growth engineer and AI engineer are not BLS occupations** — no SOC code, no OOH page, no OEWS wage/employment table. Confirmed via BLS's own SOC-code-lookup guidance and a tech-immigration SOC-mapping guide (both in sources.md). This is not a research miss; the data does not exist. For the interactive, this is itself a good beat ("two of these four job titles aren't even real to the federal government yet") but it means growth_engineer/ai_engineer in data.json carry only job-posting and survey signal, never a BLS wage/growth number — don't let the viz imply parity of data quality across all four roles.

## Industry-vertical coverage is incomplete vs. the assignment's list
The assignment asks for pay by vertical across healthcare, finance/insurance, tech/software, government, retail, education, manufacturing. BLS OOH's Work Environment/Pay tabs only surface each occupation's **top 5 industries by employment**, not a full NAICS table:
- Statisticians' top 5: federal government, R&D services, healthcare, state colleges/universities, computer systems design. **No retail, no manufacturing, no finance/insurance industry showed up in the top 5** — BLS may simply employ too few statisticians in those sectors to publish a reliable estimate.
- Data scientists' top 5: publishing/broadcasting, insurance carriers, computer systems design, management of companies, credit intermediation. **No healthcare, no government, no retail, no education, no manufacturing** in the top 5.
- I tried to reach the full OEWS NAICS industry tables directly (`bls.gov/oes/current/oes152041.htm`, `bls.gov/oes/2025/may/oes152041.htm`, and the 15-2051 equivalents) four separate times; every attempt resolved to a generic BLS OES homepage/index page rather than the occupation-specific industry table, most likely because BLS's granular industry cross-tabs now live only behind the interactive `data.bls.gov/oes/` query tool (JS-driven, not a static page WebFetch can read). **If the visualization needs healthcare/retail/education/manufacturing figures specifically, someone will need to pull them from that interactive tool by hand** — flagging rather than fabricating.
- CareerOneStop (a DOL-sponsored mirror that sometimes exposes this more cleanly) returned **HTTP 403** on fetch.

## Numbers that disagree with each other (real disagreements, not errors)
1. **Employment counts, OOH vs. OEWS news release, same occupation-year:** Statisticians 31,300 (OOH) vs 29,030 (OEWS Table 1) — a 7.8% gap. Data scientists 275,600 (OOH) vs 262,440 (OEWS Table 1) — a 5.0% gap. Same pattern for mathematicians (2,200 vs 2,030) and CIRS (38,600 vs 37,200). This is a known feature of BLS's own data ecosystem — OOH employment figures come from the BLS Employment Projections program, which adjusts the raw OEWS survey estimate. **Both numbers are legitimate BLS figures; they are simply not the same product.** data.json carries both, labeled.
2. **Data scientist pay, off by ~2x depending on the survey:** Stack Overflow 2025's *global* sample says $82,910 median; a US-only re-cut of that same 2025 dataset (via techrecruiting.io, a secondary source, not Stack Overflow itself) says $160,000; BLS's national OEWS figure says $120,230. All three are "2025 data scientist median pay" and all three are defensible — they differ by geography mix and by what counts as "data scientist" in a self-reported developer survey vs. a government occupational classification. **Do not present a single "the" data-scientist salary figure without naming which of these three you mean.**
3. **O*NET education-distribution percentages don't visibly sum to 100.** Statisticians: 62% master's + 14% bachelor's + 10% doctoral = 86%, leaving 14% unaccounted (likely spread across associate's/some-college/professional-degree buckets O*NET tracks but the fetch summary didn't surface). Data scientists: 48% bachelor's + 44% master's = 92%, 8% unaccounted. **Treat these as directionally correct (statisticians skew noticeably more graduate-credentialed) but re-verify the exact remaining buckets against O*NET's raw table before building a precise donut/stacked-bar chart from them.**

## Job-board count quality, board by board
- **Indeed**: counts are read off the page `<title>`, which Indeed visibly **rounds/buckets** rather than showing a live exact count (e.g. "1,000 Statistician Jobs", "3,000 Ai Engineer Jobs" — round numbers, almost certainly display buckets, not literal counts of 1,000/3,000 postings). The "data scientist" query didn't even render a number in its title — likely because volume exceeds whatever threshold triggers Indeed's numbered-title template. **Treat all four Indeed numbers as order-of-magnitude, not exact.**
- **Built In**: counts here ARE exact — obtained by paging through live results and counting listings by hand (25/page, confirmed against two different queries). But this run's result set for all four queries skewed heavily toward **Binance and Philippines/APAC postings** — e.g. 18 of "data scientist"'s 32 hits were Binance alone. That's a property of Built In's current index/crawl, not a US-market signal; don't present Built In's raw counts as "US startup demand" without that caveat.
- **Wellfound**: role-specific landing pages (`/role/r/{slug}`) only exist for titles Wellfound tags as a distinct role. **"statistician" and "growth-engineer" have no such page** — navigating to either silently redirects to the unfiltered "any role, remote" firehose (11,640 listings), which I did NOT record as a per-role count (would be meaningless). Only data-scientist (161) and AI-engineer (1,985) counts are usable, and both are **remote-only** by Wellfound's default filter, not total postings.
- One Built In page load (AI engineer, page 5, first attempt) **unexpectedly redirected the tab to a LinkedIn sign-in/sign-up page**, almost certainly a rogue ad/redirect on Built In's side rather than anything in this workflow. No credentials or account info were entered at any point; the tool itself flagged LinkedIn as "not approved for tool access" and the redirect was abandoned immediately in favor of a fresh tab. Documenting for awareness if Built In is scraped again.

## Not independently re-verified (time-box cutoff)
- Burtch Works, O'Reilly, and Levels.fyi figures each came from a single fetch/search pass, not triangulated against a second independent source the way several BLS/O*NET figures were (e.g., data scientist median pay of $120,230 appeared identically in three separate fetches — OOH page, O*NET summary, O*NET wage table — which is why it's flagged "High" confidence rather than "Medium"). Figures resting on one source only are marked accordingly in shock-stats.md; none are fabricated, but none should be quoted as BLS-grade precision either.
- O'Reilly's dedicated salary-survey series could not be confirmed beyond its 2021 edition; a 2025 "Technology Trends" report exists but is a platform-usage study, not a compensation survey. If the post specifically needs a 2024/2025 O'Reilly salary figure, it likely doesn't exist — don't substitute the 2021 number without labeling its age.
