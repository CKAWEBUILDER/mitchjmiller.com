## Caught a likely-fabricated stat — do not use
A claim reading roughly "Semrush study of 18.4 billion categorized queries across 142 countries found informational intent = 57.3% of all searches, up from 52%, transactional +19% YoY, navigational -6% YoY" appears **verbatim, word-for-word identical**, across multiple AI-generated "SEO statistics roundup" sites (amraandelma.com, omnibound.ai, others). There is no article on semrush.com/blog matching it, no author, no methodology, and no other outlet independently reporting it. Direct search for the exact figure "57.3%" + Semrush turns up nothing but the same copy-pasted paragraph. This has the signature of an AI-hallucinated statistic that has been laundered into apparent consensus by content-mill sites citing each other. **Excluded from data.json and shock-stats.md. Do not use this figure or cite Semrush for a "18.4 billion queries" study.**

A second, smaller catch: one search summary claimed "Perplexity averages 8.2 sources per answer" and, in the same paragraph, "Perplexity averages 21.9 citations per response" — internally contradictory. Also excluded.

## Blocked / unreliable fetches
- **Google**: fully blocked on every attempt. Returned a "having trouble accessing Google Search" interstitial instead of results. Expected per the assignment brief.
- **DuckDuckGo** (`html.duckduckgo.com/html/`): blocked by a CAPTCHA ("select all squares containing a duck") on every attempt. Did not attempt to solve it (CAPTCHA-bypassing is off-limits).
- **Bing**: the one usable live-fetch source, but unreliable for 6 of 10 queries tried. Two distinct failure modes observed, both worth flagging to Mitchell since they matter for any future "live SERP checker" feature:
  1. **Query truncation**: for several multi-word queries, Bing (as rendered by this session's fetch tool) appears to have parsed only the *first word* and returned dictionary-definition results for it — `what causes inflation` → results about "cause"; `best project management software 2026` / `best running shoes 2026` → results about "best"; `sign up for spotify premium` → results about "sign." Did not reproduce for short/brand/comparison queries (`youtube`, `chase bank login`, `ahrefs vs semrush`, `how to tie a tie` all worked cleanly).
  2. **Non-US geolocation**: `nike air max 270`, `cheap flights to honolulu`, `plumber near me`, and `dentist in austin texas` all returned Philippines-based results (`.ph` domains, PRC/Philippines licensing content, PHP pricing) regardless of an explicit `&mkt=en-US&cc=US` parameter. This suggests the fetch infrastructure's outbound IP is Southeast-Asia-geolocated and Bing is honoring IP geography over query parameters for at least some modules.
  - **Net effect**: no shopping carousel, local pack, ads, or AI/Copilot answer box was visible in *any* of the 10 Bing fetches — including on queries with well-documented high trigger rates (e.g., `ahrefs vs semrush`, a comparison query that independently-studied data says triggers an AI Overview 95.4% of the time). This is consistent with a simpler, separate explanation that also applies: WebFetch converts static HTML to markdown with **no JavaScript execution**, and AI answer boxes / shopping units / local packs are frequently JS-rendered. **Recommendation for the write-up: describe organic ranking order from these fetches as real and dated; describe AI Overview / shopping / local-pack / ad presence using the aggregate study data in data.json, not as something directly observed.** This is exactly how the examples in data.json are labelled.

## Disagreements between studies (present both, don't cherry-pick)

**"What % of searches show an AI Overview overall?"** — no single answer; three credible trackers disagree because they're measuring different keyword panels at different times:
- BrightEdge: 48% (Feb 2026 reading, 9 industry verticals, AI Catalyst/Generative Parser)
- seoClarity: ~30% (500M-keyword US desktop panel — seen via secondary reporting only)
- Semrush: doesn't publish one clean "overall %" in the pieces reviewed here — instead reports the *intent mix shift* (91.3%→57.1% informational share of AIO-triggering queries) and *industry-specific* growth rates, which is a different cut of the same underlying phenomenon.
Treat "AI Overviews appear on X% of searches" as inherently panel-dependent. If the artifact shows one number, caption it with the source and date rather than presenting it as a universal constant — this is a fast-moving, method-sensitive figure and multiple 2026 stats sites (searchlab.nl, omnibound.ai) explicitly warn about the same spread (their range: ~21%–65%).

**"Do AI Overviews meaningfully cut organic CTR?"** — Ahrefs (300K keywords, GSC-verified, -58%) and Seer Interactive (5.47M queries, -38% to -100%+ depending on citation status) both say yes, emphatically, with large disclosed samples. **First Page Sage** (undisclosed sample, "meta-analysis") claims AI Overviews have "minimal impact" on organic CTR and that mainly ad clicks suffer. This is a direct, unresolved contradiction. Given the sample-size and methodology-transparency gap, weight Ahrefs/Seer far more heavily; if First Page Sage's CTR-by-position curve is used for the plain (no-AIO) baseline numbers, don't also import its AIO-impact claim.

**"Zero-click %" vs "open-web click %"** — these get used interchangeably in casual writing but are not quite the same metric. SparkToro's 2024 report framed the finding as "374 of every 1,000 US searches send a click to the open web" (37.4% open-web click rate, which nets out clicks that stay on Google-owned properties like Maps/YouTube/Images as non-open-web but not necessarily "zero-click"). SparkToro's 2026 report instead charts a "60.45% zero-click" figure for that same 2024 baseline. Both are legitimate, both come from the same publisher, but they're not computed the same way. Pick one framing for the artifact and cite its exact source rather than blending the two.

**Perplexity's own citation-density numbers were internally inconsistent** in the source material gathered (see fabrication note above) — excluded rather than resolved.

## Gaps — could not find reliable intent-segmented data for
- **People Also Ask presence by intent.** Only found an all-query average (64.9%, Semrush, medium confidence — not directly fetched). No study broke this down informational vs. commercial vs. transactional.
- **Shopping-result (PLA/Shopping-unit) presence rate by intent**, as distinct from "AI Overviews appearing on shopping queries" (which we do have — 14% of shopping queries per one Semrush-adjacent study, and ~13-14% for "pure transactional" queries). The classic Shopping-carousel presence-by-intent stat was not located from a named, dated primary study in the time available.
- **Video-carousel presence by intent.** Have overall video presence (~38.5% of tracked results, one snapshot date) and AIO/video co-occurrence (25.65% of AIO SERPs also show a video carousel, Semrush) but not a clean by-intent breakdown.
- **AI citation patterns segmented by query type/intent** (e.g., "does ChatGPT cite Reddit more for informational vs. transactional queries?"). Every citation study found (Profound, Semrush, Tinuiti-via-secondary) segments by *platform* and *time*, not by *query intent*. This is a genuine open question in the public research as of this pass, not just something we ran out of time to find — worth saying so explicitly if the artifact implies otherwise.
- **Datos "State of Search Q1 2025" PDF** could not be fetched directly (exceeded the 10MB WebFetch content limit). The ~30%-navigational figure attributed to it is from a secondary roundup only. If this stat matters for the final piece, download the PDF directly (link in sources.md) and pull the number first-hand rather than trusting the secondary citation.
- **Google AI Mode usage scale** is reported wildly inconsistently across sources gathered in the same search batch — "100M+ users" and "1 billion+ monthly users" both appeared as 2026 figures without enough context to reconcile (possibly different points in 2026, or different definitions of "user"). Did not include a headline AI Mode user-count figure in data.json for this reason.

## Examples coverage vs. the brief
The brief asked for 8 real queries, 2 per intent (informational, navigational, commercial investigation, transactional; local optional). What's actually in data.json:
- **Informational**: 1 clean live example (`how to tie a tie`) + 1 attempted-but-blocked (`what is metabolic syndrome`, documented as a methodology finding, not invented).
- **Navigational**: 2 clean live examples (`youtube`, `chase bank login`) — full coverage.
- **Commercial investigation**: 1 clean live example (`ahrefs vs semrush`) + 1 attempted-but-blocked (`best running shoes 2026`).
- **Transactional**: 0 clean live examples — both attempts (`buy nike air max 270` / `nike air max 270 price`, `cheap flights to honolulu`) hit the geolocation failure mode. Composition described in the write-up should lean on the Seer/Semrush/Ahrefs transactional AIO-rate data (5–14% depending on study) plus the paid-click-share data (Aleyda Solis/Similarweb) rather than a fabricated screenshot.
- **Local (bonus)**: 0 clean live examples (`plumber near me`, `dentist in austin texas` both geolocation-failed) — composition should lean entirely on the Whitespark study (15% AIO / 93% local pack for local intent).
Recommend either (a) publishing the artifact with 4 confirmed live examples + 4-6 study-grounded compositions, clearly labelled per query as data.json already does, or (b) re-running the blocked queries with a real browser tool (e.g., an actual rendered-browser fetch with a US-based session) if a cleaner live screenshot is wanted before publish — flagging this as a good task for whoever has browser-automation tooling available, since it's outside this researcher's toolset (Google-blocked, DuckDuckGo CAPTCHA-blocked, Bing geo/parsing-unreliable were all confirmed, not assumed).
