## Editorial direction

Give the reader room to recognize their own opportunity. Lead with a short invitation, follow with a concrete capability, and let a relevant case or working tool provide the detail. Make the initial reading feel calm, quick and worthwhile. Occasional changes in scale, color or an interactive example may create curiosity; avoid obstructing answers, hiding material limitations or promising psychological effects.

Use one principal topic per paragraph, expressed naturally with its purpose and practical consequence. The topic map below is editorial guidance, not a repetition quota. Keep full evidence on linked, crawlable pages. Short copy and voluntary engagement are design goals; ranking gains, excitement and conversion improvements require measurement and cannot be promised.

A centered hero, narrow measure, generous desktop spacing and one hero button carry this direction. Keep secondary exploration links below the hero. Put actual brand artwork before “Strategy through delivery.” Use “Experience across” as the conservative carousel label; do not label entries as employers or imply a current endorsement. Let each logo lead to the related case where a case exists. The user describes positive professional associations; the page should show the work without inventing a testimonial.

## Learning lab: future visitor conversation concept

Status: proposed product architecture only. Keep it out of shipped-feature claims until storage, moderation, reporting and deletion work end to end. No chat backend, public room, identity collection or analytics change is authorized by this document.

The existing lab can lead with a question, let visitors vary an input, show the resulting difference and provide the method beneath it. A future discussion layer can invite the visitor to share what they noticed under a chosen name. Participation remains optional; the tools remain available without joining a chat or consenting to analytics.

Proposed entry sequence:

1. **Open discussion.** Show who can read the room, the proposed retention period, what the service records and how reporting/deletion work before sending anything.
2. **Choose a display name.** Explain that a pseudonym is visible to other participants; it is not a promise of anonymity to the service operator. Do not require email or a permanent account for the basic flow.
3. **Join voluntarily.** Separate the choice to participate in chat from analytics/cookie preferences. Do not treat cookie acceptance as agreement to expose a visitor’s identity, full browsing activity or private data.
4. **Keep control.** Give the visitor a clear leave action, report/mute controls and a way to delete their own messages. A session-bound secret can support deletion without a conventional account, with the loss-of-access consequence explained.
5. **Operate the room.** Establish a named moderation owner, a short documented retention policy, abuse controls, deletion behavior and a way to disable posting. Never claim confidentiality in a public room.

Necessary session storage, analytics, message visibility and any cross-session identity are distinct product decisions. A privacy notice must describe the actual implemented behavior. This is product planning, not a determination of legal compliance or a reproduction of Contiem’s consent implementation.

### Questions to resolve before live chat

- Who can read a message: current room participants, all site visitors, or only the operator?
- Who handles abuse while the operator is offline, and what pauses posting if no moderator is available?
- How long are messages and service logs retained, and can a visitor remove a message after losing the session?
- What useful learning outcome will discussion add that the current tools do not already provide?
