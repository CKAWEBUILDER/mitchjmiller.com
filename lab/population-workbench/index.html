Lab · Free tool · v1.0.0

Build your own simulated population

Turn a question into an inspectable population model. Filter 39.5 million Californians from public Census microdata, weigh two kinds of scenario against the people you selected, draw attribute-only personas, and export everything with its assumptions attached.

  1. Define the question. One sentence. It travels with every export so the assumptions stay attached to the decision.
  2. Filter the population. Age, personal income band, education, employment, sex and race. Every count is weighted, so it reads as people in California, not rows in a file.
  3. Compare scenarios. A verified universal-basic-income policy model, and a reach-and-adoption estimate for a product, price or budget decision where every assumption is a visible input.
  4. Draw personas. Records sampled from your selection in proportion to their survey weight, described by attributes only. Synthetic composites drawn from Census microdata. Not real people.
  5. Export. A self-contained interactive HTML report, a CSV of the personas and a JSON of every input, built in your browser.

How to read the numbers: observed comes straight from a Census record · calculated is weighted arithmetic on those records · simulated is the UBI model's output · assumed is something you typed · estimate is arithmetic on assumptions and not a forecast.

04 Draw personas

Synthetic composites drawn from Census microdata. Not real people. Each card is one record from your filtered selection, drawn with probability proportional to its survey weight, described by the attributes on the record and nothing else.

Persona cards appear when the data loads.

05 Export

Built in your browser from the current state; nothing is uploaded. Each file carries the question, filters, assumptions, results and the same labels and disclaimers shown here.

The free workbench runs on public Census microdata and labels every assumption. The same approach can run on your own records, with the population calibrated to your customers and the scenario logic validated against outcomes you have actually observed.
